# Scientific scope and limitations

These are properties of the implemented model and input assumptions. Software installation checks, synthetic tests, source hashes and Monte Carlo convergence do not establish ecological validity, regulatory recognition or accuracy for a particular assessment. Assess applicability using independent observations and suitable benchmarks. Synthetic examples are demonstrations, not calibration evidence.

## Model structure

1. **One year only.** The calculation has no multi-year stock carry-forward, succession trajectory or marginal intervention counterfactual. Fire, land-use conversion and other disturbances are not independently simulated. See [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md) and [`assess_year`](../src/open_fnrb/annual.py).
2. **Logistic growth is an assumed response.** Growth is `4 I (B/Keff) (1-B/Keff)`, with concurrent constant prescribed harvesting. It does not represent species-, age-, season- or climate-specific growth mechanisms separately. Peak increment occurs at half capacity; growth is zero at zero stock and at capacity. See [`annual_logistic`](../src/open_fnrb/growth.py).
3. **Effective capacity is adjusted to starting stock.** `Keff=max(Kref,B0)` preserves the chosen peak increment while changing the logistic coefficient. When starting stock exceeds reference capacity, growth initially becomes zero rather than modelling decline toward the original capacity. See [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md) and [`assess_year`](../src/open_fnrb/annual.py).
4. **Growth/capacity values need independent justification.** Mean forest stock is not automatically an equilibrium ceiling, and a reported annual increment is not automatically the logistic maximum. The tool does not fit or validate ecological parameters. No ecological defaults are supplied. See [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md) and [parameter inputs](INPUTS.md).
5. **Canopy-to-stock conversion is linear.** Starting biomass equals canopy fraction times reference capacity; canopy cover alone does not establish biomass density. In Monte Carlo the same sampled capacity is used for this conversion and growth, imposing a model dependence. See [`stock_from_canopy`](../src/open_fnrb/annual.py) and [`Simulation.realization`](../src/open_fnrb/simulation.py).
6. **Zero-stock behaviour is restrictive.** Exactly zero starting stock produces no growth or establishment in the annual model. Positive stock can arise from a mapped zero only through the explicitly selected uncertain-zero sampling policy. Missing values are not zeros. See [`annual_logistic`](../src/open_fnrb/growth.py) and [`sample_agb`](../src/open_fnrb/uncertainty.py).
7. **Prescribed harvesting is spatially uniform.** Estimated annual harvested biomass is applied as prescribed annual harvest, uniformly per household-accessible hectare. Household woody biomass use serves as a harvest proxy only where the sourcing assumption is justified. Exhaustion stops harvesting in a cell; unrealised harvest is not transferred to other cells. This does not simulate collector preference, changing access or displacement of harvesting to other locations. See [definitions](terminology_definitions.md) and [`assess_year`](../src/open_fnrb/annual.py).
8. **The estimate compares prescribed harvest with annual regeneration.** For positive prescribed annual harvest H, `NRB=max(0,H-R)` and `fNRB=NRB/H`, where R is modelled annual woody biomass regeneration under concurrent harvesting. H includes unrealised harvest; the denominator is not modelled realised harvest. NRB is therefore distinct from modelled stock depletion. The result is not a deforestation rate or an intervention emissions estimate. With zero prescribed harvest, zero fNRB is a flagged reporting convention; with positive prescribed harvest and no eligible resource, fNRB is one. See [definitions](terminology_definitions.md), [`assess_year`](../src/open_fnrb/annual.py) and [audit interpretation](AUDIT.md).
9. **Travel boundaries are geometric approximations.** Radius is derived from round-trip time, travelling share and speed, using straight-line/geodesic buffers. Roads, terrain, tenure and actual collection behaviour are not simulated. Eligibility and exclusions require explicit evidence. See [`collection_boundary`](../src/open_fnrb/travel.py), [input requirements](INPUTS.md) and [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md).
10. **Household allocation is an area proxy.** The built-up route allocates administrative household totals in proportion to selected built-up area inside the boundary, using the full administrative support as denominator. It does not infer occupancy or household density variation within that support. Household counts, allocation and travel assumptions stay fixed in Monte Carlo. Wet-fuel, charcoal, energy and other conversions must be justified upstream. See [`allocate_households`](../src/open_fnrb/preparation.py) and [harvest inputs](INPUTS.md).

## Uncertainty and interpretation

11. **Uncertainty distributions are prescribed.** Positive AGB and positive growth/capacity draws use arithmetic-moment-matched lognormal distributions. Canopy uses a normal distribution truncated to [0,1]. The latter's mean and SD generally differ from the input location and scale near bounds. Fixed mapped zeros omit positive uncertainty; censored-normal zeros can increase expected stock. See [`uncertainty.py`](../src/open_fnrb/uncertainty.py) and [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md).
12. **Dependence choices are limited.** Stock errors are independent across native cells or share a latent error; neither estimates a general spatial covariance model. Fragments of one native cell retain the same draw. Parameters share draws within their kind/source group, while growth and capacity groups are independent. Cross-component dependencies are not freely configurable. See [`cell_uniforms`](../src/open_fnrb/uncertainty.py) and [`Simulation.realization`](../src/open_fnrb/simulation.py).
13. **Harvest uncertainty covers only supported models.** Annual harvest is fixed, sampled from a lognormal total-harvest model, or derived through a household woody biomass use bootstrap using independent survey units or an acknowledged iid design. Household fuel-use observations are a proxy for harvested biomass under the documented sourcing assumption. The tool does not infer representativeness, weights, fuel provenance or arbitrary complex survey designs. Bootstrapping household woody biomass use does not sample household totals or spatial allocation. See [`bootstrap_household_woody_biomass_use`](../src/open_fnrb/uncertainty.py), [`Simulation._validate_harvest`](../src/open_fnrb/simulation.py) and [input requirements](INPUTS.md).
14. **Classification uncertainty changes eligibility only.** Probabilities are P(reference class | mapped class), obtained from row-normalised supplied counts. Native classification cells are sampled independently; their fragments share a class. Matrix-estimation uncertainty and spatially correlated classification errors are omitted. Ecological zones, parameter sources, exclusions and household allocation remain fixed. Inappropriate survey counts are not repaired by normalisation. See [`classification.py`](../src/open_fnrb/classification.py), [`sample_classes`](../src/open_fnrb/uncertainty.py) and [`Simulation.classification_contract`](../src/open_fnrb/simulation.py).
15. **Monte Carlo is conditional on the selected model and evidence.** It does not cover omitted processes, structural model uncertainty or every uncertain input. Boundaries, travel, exclusions, ecological geometry and household totals/allocation are not separately sampled. P10/P90 describe the central 80% of simulated outcomes; they are not automatically a confidence interval for the real-world quantity. See [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md), [input requirements](INPUTS.md) and [`simulation.py`](../src/open_fnrb/simulation.py).
16. **Numerical precision is not scientific accuracy.** Mean and percentile checks assess fNRB numerical stability, and a run may reach its iteration limit without passing. Spatial percentiles have no separate precision acceptance test. More iterations cannot repair unsupported evidence or model bias. See [`precision`](../src/open_fnrb/monte_carlo.py) and [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md).
17. **Spatial summaries have restricted meaning.** No pixel-level fNRB is calculated. Cell percentiles and SDs cannot be added to obtain overall uncertainty. Classification-enabled density maps use fixed candidate area; an ineligible contribution of zero does not imply zero physical biomass. See [audit interpretation](AUDIT.md) and [`spatial_summary.py`](../src/open_fnrb/spatial_summary.py).
18. **Input products and spatial support constrain applicability.** Matching grids, units, coverage and dates require review. Provider SD, residual error, standard error, RMSE and confidence limits are not interchangeable without evidence. Regional WGS84 and suitable projected metre CRSs are supported; antimeridian/global-width domains and other geographic datums require explicit preprocessing. Pixel size does not itself establish observation support or independent errors. See [input requirements](INPUTS.md), [raster encoding](RASTER_ENCODING.md), [`WGS84Area`](../src/open_fnrb/area.py) and [`gis_inputs.py`](../src/open_fnrb/gis_inputs.py).

The model-structure interpretations above follow the named functions and the methodology; they do not assert quantified bias for a particular site. Report the assumptions and omissions applicable to each assessment alongside its results.
